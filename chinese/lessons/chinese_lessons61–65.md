# Mandarin Chinese - Lessons 61–65

## 1. Study Guide

This study guide provides a strategic synthesis of Mandarin Chinese as it transitions from the ethereal aesthetics of the Tang Dynasty to the pragmatic complexities of modern life. By progressing from the classical _Wen Yan Wen_ (literary Chinese) of Lesson 61 to the contemporary logistics of real estate, international travel, and computer hardware in subsequent lessons, the learner develops a multidimensional fluency. This progression allows students to observe how foundational concepts—such as "ground" (_di4_) or "bright" (_ming2_)—evolve from the evocative imagery of 1,000-year-old poetry into the functional terminology of property management and hardware specifications. Mastering this spectrum ensures the learner can navigate both the cultural soul and the bureaucratic systems of the Chinese-speaking world.

**Thematic Breakdown**

- **Classical Poetry (Lesson 61):** This lesson analyzes the emotional and linguistic core of the Tang Dynasty through works by Li Bai, Meng Haoran, and Wang Zhihuan. In _Wen Yan Wen_, most words are monosyllabic (e.g., _yue4_ for moon), contrasting with the disyllabic modern Mandarin (_yue4 liang_). A key linguistic bridge is found in the poem "Quiet Night Thought," where Li Bai uses the word _yi2_ (to suspect/doubt), mistaking moonlight for frost (_shuang1_). The imagery of climbing the "Heron Lodge" (_Deng1 Guan4 Que4 Lou2_) serves as a philosophical metaphor for broadening one's perspective by reaching a higher level.
- **Modern Living & Real Estate (Lesson 62):** Navigating the Chinese housing market requires mastering the distinction between renting (_zu1_) and buying property (_mai3 fang2_). High-value concepts include the legal necessity of an income proof (_shou1ru4 zheng4ming2_) from one’s work unit (_dan1wei4_) and the financial requirement of a 30% down payment (_shou3fu4_), a standard government regulation. The term _di4_ (ground) from classical poetry here evolves into _fang2 di4 chan3_ (real estate).
- **Travel and Logistics (Lesson 63):** Proficiency in travel involves the procedural flow from the check-in counter to arrival. Learners must master the terminology for checking luggage (_tuo1 yun4_), receiving a boarding pass (_deng1 ji1 pai2_), and locating the correct gate (_deng1 ji1 kou3_). In-flight communication shifts to safety (fastening seatbelts during _dian1 bo1_ or turbulence) and specific service requests, such as drinks without ice (_bing1 kuai4_).
- **Technology & Personal Choice (Lesson 64):** This paradigm explores the choice between a brand-name PC (_pin3 pai2 ji1_), which offers quality assurance and service, and a DIY assembled PC (_zu3 zhuang1 ji1_), valued for cost-efficiency. Technical fluency is built through understanding hardware configurations (_pei4 zhi4_), specifically RAM (_nei4 cun2_), motherboards (_zhu3 ban3_), and hard drives (_ying4 pan2_). In this context, _zhao4_ specifically refers to Megabytes, with a "top-level" configuration requiring at least 1,000MB (1GB) of RAM.

By recognizing how character roots persist across these diverse contexts, learners can bridge the gap between academic study and real-world application. We will now apply this knowledge in a test environment to facilitate active recall.

## 5. Grammar Notes & Tips

Advancing in Mandarin requires a shift from isolated vocabulary to the mastery of syntax. The following patterns illustrate the versatile structures found in Lesson 65 and throughout the transcriptions.

**The Modal "dei3" (得)**

- **Pattern:** Subject + 得 + Verb + Object.
- **Meaning:** Must / Have to / Should.
- **Example:** 我得在伦敦转机。
- **Pinyin:** wo3 dei3 zai4 lun2 dun1 zhuan3 ji1.
- **Translation:** I must (have to) change planes in London.
- **Usage Notes:** Unlike _ying1 gai1_ (should), which implies a moral or logical suggestion, _dei3_ is more colloquial and indicates a situational necessity or external requirement.
- **Tips:** Use _dei3_ when the action is non-negotiable due to circumstances.

**Conditionals with "yao4 shi... jiu..." (要是... 就...)**

- **Pattern:** 要是 + Condition, (Subject) + 就 + Result.
- **Meaning:** If... then...
- **Example:** 要是下雨, 我就不来。
- **Pinyin:** yao4 shi4 xia4 yu3, wo3 jiu4 bu4 lai2.
- **Translation:** If it rains, then I won't come.
- **Usage Notes:** This is functionally identical to the _ru2 guo3... jiu..._ structure but is much more common in spoken Mandarin.
- **Tips:** You may omit the subject in the second clause if it is the same as the first.

**Continuous Aspect "zheng4 zai4" (正在)**

- **Pattern:** Subject + 正在 + Verb.
- **Meaning:** In the middle of / Currently [doing something].
- **Example:** 我正在吃饭。
- **Pinyin:** wo3 zheng4 zai4 chi1 fan4.
- **Translation:** I am currently eating.
- **Usage Notes:** This emphasizes the ongoing nature of an action at a specific moment.
- **Tips:** It is most effective with active verbs like reading (_kan4 shu1_) or sleeping (_shui4 jiao4_).

**The "Except/Besides" structure (chu2 le... yi3 wai4)**

- **Pattern:** 除了 + A + 以外, (还/都) + B.
- **Meaning:** Besides A, there is also B / Except for A, all are B.
- **Example:** 除了每个月还贷款以外, 还有物业管理费。
- **Pinyin:** chu2 le mei3 ge4 yue4 huan2 dai4 kuan3 yi3 wai4, hai2 you3 wu4 ye4 guan3 li3 fei4.
- **Translation:** Besides the monthly mortgage repayment, there is also a property management fee.
- **Usage Notes:** If _hai2 you3_ (also has) follows, it means "besides" (addition). If _dou1_ (all) follows, it means "except for" (exclusion).
- **Tips:** Use this to list additional costs or specific exceptions in a group.

**Measure Words (Classifiers)**

- **Pattern:** Number + Measure Word + Noun.
- **Meaning:** Required classifiers for nouns.
- **Usage Detail:**
  - **张 (zhang1):** For flat objects, like beds (_yi1 zhang1 chuang2_).
  - **台 (tai2):** For machines/appliances, like computers (_yi1 tai2 dian4 nao3_).
  - **辆 (liang4):** For wheeled vehicles, like bicycles (_yi1 liang4 zi4 xing2 che1_).
  - **套 (tao4):** For sets or apartments (_yi1 tao4 gong1 yu4_).
- **Tips:** Always memorize the measure word as an integral part of the noun phrase.
