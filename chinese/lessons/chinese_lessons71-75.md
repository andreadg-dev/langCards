# Mandarin Chinese - Lessons 71-75

## 1. Study Guide

As a student of Mandarin Chinese, the transition from basic survival phrases to specialized vocabulary in religion, public services, and legal frameworks marks a pivotal shift in your linguistic development. Lessons 71-75 serve as a pedagogical bridge, moving you beyond the "what" of daily life into the "how" and "why" of Chinese cultural and institutional logic. By mastering this set, you evolve from a passive observer of the language into a sophisticated participant capable of navigating complex bureaucratic systems and discussing the philosophical underpinnings of Chinese society.

**Religious Foundations: Taoism vs. Buddhism**

Lesson 71 contrasts the two most influential spiritual pillars in China. Our analysis focuses not just on their existence, but on their distinct views of reality:

- **Taoism (道教 [dao4 jiao4]):** China’s native religion. It is inherently world-focused, teaching that through specific practices or cultivation (**修炼 [xiu1 lian4]**), one can achieve immortality (**长生不老 [chang2 sheng1 bu4 lao3]**) in the here and now.
- **Buddhism (佛教 [fo2 jiao4]):** An imported Indian faith that became deeply "sinicized." Crucially, the source notes that Buddhism views reality as cruel (**残酷 [can2 ku4]**) and hopeless. Because believers feel they cannot change their current suffering, they abandon worldly desires to focus entirely on the "future world."

**Public Inquiries and Logistics**

Lessons 72-74 detail the technical procedures for navigating modern Chinese institutions.

- **Telecommunications:** Lesson 72 highlights a significant logistical hurdle: the "out-of-town" phone (**外地的手机 [wai4 di4 de shou3 ji1]**). Certain inquiry hotlines (like the 114 navigation service) may be inaccessible if your SIM card is registered in a different city, requiring you to find alternative service numbers like the Li Suli hotline.
- **The Dining Reservation:** Booking a table at a premier establishment like "Xiao Nan Guo" involves specific data points. You must provide a callback number, specify seating (such as the lobby/main floor—**大堂 [da4 tang2]**), and perform a character breakdown of your name to avoid homophone errors.

**Legal and Safety Frameworks**

Lesson 75 introduces the legal linguistic markers of traffic safety. The overarching pedagogical theme is the concept of "responsibility for self and others" (**为自己和他人负责 [wei4 zi4 ji3 he2 ta1 ren2 fu4 ze2]**). The curriculum focuses on identifying violations such as speeding (**超速 [chao1 su4]**) and running red lights (**闯红灯 [chuang3 hong2 deng1]**), while emphasizing the linguistic rhythm of safety mnemonics used by the general public.

In summary, these themes reflect the multifaceted complexity of modern Chinese social and institutional life. We will now move into the assessment phase to test your mastery of these domains.

## 5. Grammar Notes & Tips

To achieve the level of precision required for formal inquiries or legal reporting, you must master resultative complements and directional particles.

**1. 通过...就... (By means of / through... then...)**

- **Meaning:** Indicates a result attained via a specific method or process.
- **Source Example:** **通过**修炼**就**可以达到长生不老。
- **Pinyin:** [tong1 guo4 xiu1 lian4 jiu4 ke3 yi3 da2 dao4 chang2 sheng1 bu4 lao3]
- **Explanation:** Through cultivation, one can then reach immortality.
- **Usage Note:** "**通过**" introduces the necessary condition or method.

**2. 为...负责 (To be responsible for...)**

- **Meaning:** This structure identifies the object of responsibility.
- **Source Example:** 要**为**自己和他人**负责**的。
- **Pinyin:** [yao4 wei4 zi4 ji3 he2 ta1 ren2 fu4 ze2 de]
- **Explanation:** (One) must be responsible for oneself and others.
- **Common Mistake:** Avoid omitting "**为**." In Chinese, "responsible" is not a simple adjective as in "I am responsible." You must always use the "**为** + Object + **负责**" structure.

**3. 打...折 (To give a discount)**

- **Meaning:** Describes the percentage of the original price that is _paid_.
- **Source Example:** 其他是**打8.5折**的。
- **Pinyin:** [qi2 ta1 shi4 da3 ba1 dian3 wu3 zhe2 de]
- **Explanation:** Everything else is 15% off (you pay 85%).
- **Common Mistake:** Western learners often assume "**8折**" means 80% off. In fact, it means you pay 80% of the price (a 20% discount). The higher the number, the smaller the discount.

**4. 来得及 / 来不及 (To have enough time / not have enough time)**

- **Meaning:** Expresses the capability to complete an action within a time limit.
- **Source Example:** 我没**来得及**记下来。
- **Pinyin:** [wo3 mei2 lai2 de ji2 ji4 xia4 lai2]
- **Explanation:** I didn't have enough time to write (it) down.
- **Usage Note:** Often used in high-stress contexts, such as recording a license plate before a car drives away.

**5. V + 不到 (Resultative verb indicating inability)**

- **Meaning:** Indicates that the intended result of an action cannot be achieved.
- **Source Example:** 这我帮您**看不到**。
- **Pinyin:** [zhe4 wo3 bang1 nin2 kan4 bu2 dao4]
- **Explanation:** I (the operator) cannot see that (the map/info) for you.
- **Usage Note:** In Lesson 72, the operator uses "**看不到**" to explain that their system doesn't have the visual data requested. "**办不到**" is used by the police in Lesson 75 to say "I cannot do that" when asked for a bribe or favor.
