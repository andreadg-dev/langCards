# Mandarin Chinese - Lessons 31–35

## 1. Study Guide

The strategic grouping of language lessons is a cornerstone of pedagogical efficiency, moving beyond rote memorization toward contextual fluency. This module (Lessons 31–35) is designed to bridge the gap between the private domestic sphere and the public professional world. In Chinese culture, the concept of **味道 [wei4 dao4]**—encompassing both taste and smell—serves as a vital "social lubricant." Whether one is managing a household or negotiating an international trade contract at a business banquet (_jiǔ jú_), understanding sensory preferences, emotional nuances, and the mechanics of bargaining creates a holistic foundation for navigating Chinese society.

**Sensory Experiences and Culinary Culture (Lesson 31)**

In Mandarin, **味道 [wei4 dao4]** is a versatile term covering both flavor and scent. Understanding this duality is key to interpreting questions like **“这是什么味道?” [zhe4 shi4 shen2 me5 wei4 dao4?]**, which can inquire about a dish or a fragrance. The primary tastes defined in this unit are:

- **甜 [tian2]** (sweet)
- **咸 [xian2]** (salty)
- **酸 [suan1]** (sour)
- **辣 [la4]** (spicy/hot)
- **苦 [ku3]** (bitter)
- **香 [xiang1]** (fragrant)
- **臭 [chou4]** (stinky)

These preferences are systematized through the regional proverb: **“南甜, 北咸, 东酸, 西辣” [nan2 tian2, bei3 xian2, dong1 suan1, xi1 la4]**. This framework provides a cultural map: the South favors sweetness, the North prefers saltiness, the East (such as Jiangsu/Zhejiang) leans toward sour profiles, and the West (Sichuan/Hunan) is famous for heat.

**Domestic Management and Household Duties (Lesson 32)**

Managing a Chinese household often involves coordinating with an **阿姨 [a1 yi2]** (literally "auntie," but used professionally for a housekeeper). Efficiency in this sphere requires precise vocabulary for modern appliances like the **洗衣机 [xi3 yi1 ji1]** (washing machine) and tasks such as **拖地板 [tuo1 di4 ban3]** (mopping floors) or **烫衣服 [tang4 yi1 fu5]** (ironing). Food safety protocol is equally emphasized; one must distinguish between **新鲜 [xin1 xian1]** (fresh) items and those that are **过期 [guo4 qi1]** (expired), which must be **丢掉 [diu1 diao4]** (thrown away).

**Lyrical Sentiment and Emotional Expression (Lesson 33)**

The song **“你怎么说” [ni3 zen3 me5 shuo1]** offers a sophisticated look at emotional betrayal through temporal markers. Phrases like **“一年多” [yi1 nian2 duo1]** (more than a year) and **“三百六十五个日子” [san1 bai3 liu4 shi2 wu3 ge4 ri4 zi5]** (365 days) demonstrate how specific time durations are used to articulate longing. Vocabulary like **忘记 [wang4 ji4]** (forget) and **骗 [pian4]** (lie/cheat) are central to this lyrical narrative of a relationship in distress.

**Professional Trade and Factory Operations (Lesson 34)**

Transitioning to the professional sphere, Lesson 34 focuses on **国际贸易 [guo2 ji4 mao4 yi4]** (international trade). Business interactions are categorized into three phases:

1. **Inquiry:** Establishing the **业务范围 [ye4 wu4 fan4 wei2]** (business scope) and reviewing the **产品目录 [chan3 pin3 mu4 lu4]** (product catalog).
2. **Logistics:** Navigating **进口 [jin4 kou3]** (import) and **出口 [chu4 kou3]** (export), specifically ordering by the **货柜 [huo4 gui4]** (container).
3. **Finance:** Requesting a **报价 [bao4 jia4]** (quotation) and negotiating the **订金 [ding4 jin1]** (down payment), which typically starts at 30%.

**The Mechanics of Negotiation (Lesson 35)**

The art of **讨价还价 [tao3 jia4 huan2 jia4]** (bargaining) or **讲价钱 [jiang3 jia4 qian2]** (talking price) is a structured linguistic dance. It involves specific maneuvers: rejecting the initial price as **太贵了 [tai4 gui4 le5]**, requesting a discount (**可不可以便宜一点 [ke3 bu4 ke3 yi3 pian2 yi5 yi1 dian3]**), and using the "walk-away" tactic (**我要去别的地方看看 [wo3 yao4 qu4 bie2 de5 di4 fang1 kan4 kan5]**) to secure a lower price.

Mastering these thematic areas ensures that a learner is equally equipped for the intimate dinner table and the high-stakes boardroom, navigating both the private and public spheres of a Chinese-speaking environment.

## 3. Answer Key

The importance of self-correction cannot be overstated; modeling high-quality output allows you to refine your grammar and align your vocabulary usage with the precise definitions found in the source material.

1. In Mandarin, **味道 [wei4 dao4]** refers to both taste and smell. This means a single word covers sensory input from both the tongue and the nose, such as in the phrase **“这是什么味道?” [zhe4 shi4 shen2 me5 wei4 dao4?]**.
2. According to the proverb, people in the East (**东 [dong1]**) prefer **酸 [suan1]** (sour) flavors, while those in the North (**北 [bei3]**) prefer **咸 [xian2]** (salty) food.
3. Delicate items should be **用手洗 [yong4 shou3 xi3]** (hand washed), whereas the rest (**其他的 [qi2 ta1 de5]**) can be placed in the **洗衣机 [xi3 yi1 ji1]** (washing machine).
4. Items that have **过期 [guo4 qi1]** (expired) should be **丢掉 [diu1 diao4]** (thrown away). The specific example used in the dialogue is **牛奶 [niu2 nai3]** (milk).
5. The singer proves the person is lying because **“连名字你都说错” [lian2 ming2 zi5 ni3 dou1 shuo1 cuo4]** (you even said my name wrong). This failure of basic memory serves as proof of betrayal.
6. To express "more than a year," you use the structure **“一年多” [yi1 nian2 duo1]**. The word **多 [duo1]** is placed after the time measurement to indicate "more than" or "plus."
7. **业务范围 [ye4 wu4 fan4 wei2]** refers to the scope or area of a company's operations. An example from the source is a company whose scope is **出口家具 [chu1 kou3 jia1 ju4]** (exporting furniture).
8. The required **订金 [ding4 jin1]** (down payment) is **百分之三十 [bai3 fen1 zhi1 san1 shi2]**, or thirty percent.
9. The process involves asking the price (**多少钱 [duo1 shao3 qian2]**), rejecting it as too expensive (**太贵了 [tai4 gui4 le5]**), and then threatening to look elsewhere (**去别的地方看看 [qu4 bie2 de5 di4 fang1 kan4 kan5]**) to trigger a price drop.
10. **质量 [zhi4 liang4]** (quality) is the fundamental standard for a product's value, whereas **风格 [feng1 ge2]** (style), such as European classical, is a matter of aesthetic preference.

## 5. Grammar Notes & Tips

Structural patterns serve as the "scaffolding" for the vocabulary previously studied, allowing for the construction of complex, multi-layered thoughts.

**1. The '把' [ba3] Construction**

- **Pattern:** Subject + 把 + Object + Verb + Result/Direction.
- **Meaning:** Indicates how a specific object is handled, disposed of, or transferred.
- **Example:** 把我的爱情还给我 [ba3 wo3 de5 ai4 qing2 huan2 gei3 wo3].
- **Translation:** Give me back my love (Take my love and return it).
- **Senior Specialist Tip:** The '把' construction is used when the verb has a clear effect on the object. In Lesson 33, it emphasizes the _transfer_ of love back to the speaker.

**2. The '连...都' [lian2...dou1] Structure**

- **Pattern:** 连 + Subject/Noun + 都 + Verb.
- **Meaning:** Even [Noun]... (emphasizing an unexpected or extreme case).
- **Example:** 连名字你都说错 [lian2 ming2 zi5 ni3 dou1 shuo1 cuo4].
- **Translation:** You even said the name wrong.
- **Senior Specialist Tip:** While _lián_ can sometimes be omitted in casual speech, including it adds significant rhetorical weight to your disbelief or emphasis.

**3. Degree Complement '极了' [ji2 le5]**

- **Pattern:** Adjective + 极了.
- **Meaning:** Extremely [Adjective].
- **Example:** 味道好极了 [wei4 dao4 hao3 ji2 le5].
- **Translation:** The taste is extremely good.
- **Senior Specialist Tip:** Use '极了' to express an absolute limit. It is much stronger than '很' [hen3] (very) or '非常' [fei1 chang2] (extremely).

**4. Causality '因为...所以' [yin1 wei4...suo3 yi3]**

- **Pattern:** 因为 [Reason], 所以 [Result].
- **Meaning:** Because [X], therefore [Y].
- **Example:** 因为很脏, 所以你需要拖地板 [yin1 wei4 hen3 zang1, suo3 yi3 ni3 xu1 yao4 tuo1 di4 ban3].
- **Translation:** Because it is dirty, you need to mop the floor.
- **Senior Specialist Tip:** In Mandarin, it is common and often preferred to state both the cause and the result markers, whereas in English we usually only use one.

**5. Resultative Complements (懂 [dong3] vs. 错 [cuo4])**

- **Pattern:** Verb + Resultative Word.
- **Meaning:** Indicates the _attainment_ or outcome of an action.
- **Example 1:** 听懂 [ting1 dong3] — To hear and successfully understand.
- **Example 2:** 说错 [shuo1 cuo4] — To say something and make a mistake.
- **Senior Specialist Tip:** These are not just suffixes; they clarify the success of the verb. If you _ting1_ (hear) but don't _dong3_ (understand), the communication failed.

**6. Temporal Structure '一...就' [yi1...jiu4]**

- **Pattern:** 一 + Action 1 + 就 + Action 2/Result.
- **Meaning:** As soon as [Action 1] happens, [Action 2] follows immediately.
- **Example:** 我一看就知道 [wo3 yi1 kan4 jiu4 zhi1 dao4].
- **Translation:** As soon as I looked, I knew.
- **Senior Specialist Tip:** Use this to show a cause-and-effect relationship that happens almost instantaneously.
