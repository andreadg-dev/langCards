# Mandarin Chinese - Lessons 76–80

## 1. Study Guide: Thematic Review

The progression through Lessons 76–80 represents a strategic milestone in Mandarin pedagogy, marking the transition from articulating isolated actions to describing complex continuous states and navigating high-stakes social and domestic environments. These lessons move the learner beyond basic survival Chinese into household management, health-related persuasion, and the sophisticated etiquette of Chinese dining rituals. By mastering these modules, students develop the ability to describe not just the "what," but the "how" and the "duration" of actions, while simultaneously managing the logistical and interpersonal demands of daily life.

The curriculum is synthesized into four thematic pillars:

- **The Mechanics of Continuity:** The lessons introduce critical linguistic tools for expressing duration and successful persistence. The suffix **xia4 lai2** is used with verbs to indicate that an action has reached a successful outcome and its state will persist. Key pedagogical examples include **shou4 xia4 lai2** (to slim down successfully), **ting2 xia4 lai2** (to stop and stay stopped), **jie2 xia4 lai2** (to successfully connect sentences/context), **huo2 xia4 lai2** (to survive), and **ji4 xia4 lai2** (to successfully write down/record). Furthermore, the particle **zhe** (neutral tone) is established as the primary mechanism for simultaneous actions, allowing speakers to describe one action as the background state for another, such as **kan4 zhe shu1 chi1 fan4** (eating while reading).
- **Persuasion and Command:** Effective communication requires the ability to influence behavior. These lessons evaluate tools for gentle persuasion using **bie2** (bie2) to discourage harmful habits like smoking (**jie4 yan1**), often paired with "scare tactics" like the threat of **ai2 zheng4** (cancer). Conversely, the curriculum introduces high-pressure imperative structures. The phrase **gan3 jin3 ge3i wo3** (gan3 jin3 ge3i wo3) is highlighted as a forceful command used to demand immediate action in the speaker’s presence, such as finishing homework or applying brakes.
- **Domestic Navigation:** A significant portion of the lexicon is dedicated to the functional zones of the home. In the washroom, learners must distinguish between nouns like **ya2 shua1** (toothbrush) and the associated verb-object **shua1 ya2** (to brush teeth). In the kitchen, the focus shifts to methods of preparation, contrasting the use of a **lu2 zi** (stove) for boiling or brewing (**zhu3**)—which risks burning (**shao1 jiao1**) if forgotten—with the **wei1 bo1 lu2** (microwave) for convenient heating (**jia1 re4**).
- **Social Dining Etiquette:** Socially, the lessons explore the cultural "negotiation" of paying the bill (**mai3 dan1**). This involves the vocabulary of hospitality, such as **qing3 ke4** (to treat someone) and **po4 fei4** (to spend money/let someone spend), alongside "face-saving" phrases that downplay one's own generosity, such as **zhe4 suan4 shen2 me** (it’s nothing).

These thematic pillars provide the structural foundation necessary for the practical application of language tested in the following quiz.

## 2. Short-Answer Quiz

This quiz evaluates your ability to apply grammar patterns and vocabulary in context, focusing on functional usage and linguistic nuance.

3. In a social dining context, what does the expression **zhe4 suan4 shen2 me** [这算什么; zhe4 suan4 shen2 me] signify when a host is complimented on their generosity?
4. Based on Lesson 76, what is the specific grammatical function of the directional suffix **xia4 lai2** [下来; xia4 lai2] when attached to the verb **huo2** [活; huo2]?
5. Explain the difference between using a stove (**lu2 zi** [炉子; lu2 zi]) and a microwave (**wei1 bo1 lu2** [微波炉; wei1 bo1 lu2]) for heating liquids according to the cautionary advice in Lesson 79.
6. How does the phrase **gan3 jin3 ge3i wo3** [赶紧给我; gan3 jin3 ge3i wo3] change the tone of a command compared to using just **gan3 jin3** [赶紧; gan3 jin3]?
7. What does the expression **bu4 jian4 bu4 san4** [不见不散; bu4 jian4 bu4 san4] mean when confirming a meeting time at a restaurant?
8. When a speaker says **wo3 gou4 bu4 zhao2** [我够不着; wo3 gou4 bu4 zhao2] in the bathroom, what physical limitation are they describing?
9. According to Lesson 77, what specific health symptoms or conditions might a speaker use to "scare" (**xia4 hu** [吓唬; xia4 hu]) someone into quitting smoking?
10. If a guest says **rang4 ni3 po4 fei4 le** [让你破费了; rang4 ni3 po4 fei4 le] at the end of a meal, what specific social acknowledgement are they making?

## 3. Answer Key

1. You would use **bie2** followed by the verb, such as **ni3 bie2 chou1 le** (don't smoke anymore), to discourage the habit. Adding **dui4 ni3 de shen1 ti3 bu4 hao3** provides a health-based justification, emphasizing that the habit is physically harmful to the person to strengthen the persuasion.
2. **Ya2 shua1** is the noun for the physical object (a toothbrush), whereas **shua1 ya2** is the verb-object construction meaning "to brush teeth." It is a common pitfall to confuse them; pedagogical instruction emphasizes that one uses a **ya2 shua1** to **shua1 ya2**.
3. The expression translates to "What does this count as?" or "This is nothing." It is used to show modesty and downplay the host's effort or expense, indicating that the treat is a small matter compared to the friendship.
4. The suffix **xia4 lai2** indicates that the action of surviving (**huo2**) has been successfully accomplished and that the resulting state of being alive will continue. It implies a transition from a point of danger to a successful, ongoing state.
5. A **lu2 zi** (stove) is used for boiling (**zhu3**) but requires constant attention to prevent burning or overflowing, as seen with the milk example. The **wei1 bo1 lu2** (microwave) is presented as a safer, more convenient alternative for simple heating (**jia1 re4**) because it is less likely to result in a "burning smell."
6. While **gan3 jin3** simply means "immediately," adding **ge3i wo3** creates a very strong imperative that demands the action be done "for" or "in front of" the speaker. It conveys a sense of authority or extreme urgency, such as a parent ordering a child to finish homework.
7. **Bu4 jian4 bu4 san4** literally means "not seeing [each other], not dispersing." It is a firm commitment to wait for the other person at the designated spot, effectively meaning "be there or be square."
8. The speaker is describing a situation where an object, such as a towel (**mao2 jin1**), is physically out of their reach. The resultative **bu4 zhao2** indicates that despite the effort of reaching (**gou4**), the target was not reached.
9. The speaker might use the threat of **ai2 zheng4** (cancer) to scare the smoker. They may also point to immediate physical evidence, such as the person's voice becoming hoarse or harsh (**ya3**).
10. This phrase acknowledges that the host has gone to considerable expense for the guest, literally meaning "I've let you spend money." It is the standard polite way to express gratitude for a meal or a treat in Chinese culture.

## 5. Grammar Notes & Tips

These structures are the building blocks for moving beyond fragmented phrases toward sophisticated Mandarin.

**1. Resultative Suffix: -xia4 lai2**

- **Meaning:** Indicates that an action was successful and the resulting state continues.
- **Pattern:** **Verb + 下来**
- **Example:** 停下来
- **Pinyin:** ting2 xia4 lai2
- **Translation:** To stop (and stay stopped).
- **Usage Notes:** Common with "sitting down" (**zuo4 xia4 lai2**), "slimming down" (**shou4 xia4 lai2**), or "writing down" (**ji4 xia4 lai2**).

**2. Directional Resultative: -bu4 dao4 / -bu4 zhao2**

- **Meaning:** Indicates that the action failed to reach its physical or metaphorical target.
- **Pattern:** **Verb + 不到 / 不着**
- **Example:** 我听不到你说什么。
- **Pinyin:** wo3 ting1 bu4 dao4 ni3 shuo1 shen2 me.
- **Translation:** I can't hear what you are saying.
- **Usage Notes:** **bu4 dao4** often refers to sensory reach (hearing/seeing), while **bu4 zhao2** often refers to physical reach (grabbing an object).

**3. Continuous Particle: -zhe**

- **Meaning:** Placed after a verb to show a continuous state or a background action while another occurs.
- **Pattern:** **Verb 1 + 着 + (Object) + Verb 2**
- **Example:** 他笑着跟我说。
- **Pinyin:** ta1 xiao4 zhe gen1 wo3 shuo1.
- **Translation:** He said to me while smiling.
- **Usage Notes:** The first verb (**xiao4**) describes the _manner_ in which the second verb (**shuo1**) is performed.

**4. Persuasion with -bie2**

- **Meaning:** Used to persuade or order someone not to do something.
- **Pattern:** **别 + Verb + 了**
- **Example:** 你别抽了。
- **Pinyin:** ni3 bie2 chou1 le.
- **Translation:** Don't smoke anymore.
- **Usage Notes:** Adding **le** at the end implies "stopping" an action already in progress or a change in state.

**5. Imperative: gan3 jin3 ge3i wo3**

- **Meaning:** A strong, imperative command to do something immediately in the speaker's presence.
- **Pattern:** **赶紧给我 + Verb**
- **Example:** 你赶紧给我把作业做完。
- **Pinyin:** ni3 gan3 jin3 ge3i wo3 ba3 zuo4 ye4 zuo4 wan2.
- **Translation:** You finish your homework immediately!
- **Usage Notes:** Use with caution. It implies significant authority or extreme urgency.
